Four ways to do Fazi’s settings

Every mockup runs on the same engine (shared/settings-core.js): the same settings, the same shortcut recorder, cleanup rules, pinned folders, search, and reset. Only the layout and styling change, so each design behaves identically. Settings persist between designs, so a change you make in one shows up in the others.

Hover a screenshot for a second state. In each design, the bar above the window switches designs and theme, opens or closes settings, and resets the demo.

Sheet A

The closest step from today. Settings stays a modal over the window, but it is larger and grouped into inset cards like macOS System Settings. Each section has a tinted icon and a count of what you changed, and Appearance opens with a live preview.

Navigation
Sidebar list; ⌘↑ and ⌘↓ step through sections
Strength
Familiar to every Mac user; lowest build cost
Tradeoff
Covers the window, so you see Appearance changes in the preview, then in the app after closing
Open Sheet

Tab B

Settings opens as a tab in the rail, like a browser’s settings page. Nothing is modal: the sidebar, tabs, and strip stay live, so theme, accent, row height, pinned folders, Trash, and the sidebar side change in the real window as you edit. Sections run along the top of the card.

Navigation
Section bar in the card; Settings › Section in the address field; search replaces the view controls
Strength
Fits the Rail and its vertical tabs; every change is visible in place
Tradeoff
⌘, opens a tab, which is less conventional; ⌘W closes it
Open Tab

Page C

Every setting on one scrolling page, like a well-made preferences document. A contents column tracks where you are and jumps between sections. Rows are a two-column form, with label and explanation on the left and the control on the right.

Navigation
Scroll, or the contents column; ⌘↑ and ⌘↓ jump sections
Strength
Easiest to scan and to compare related settings across sections
Tradeoff
A long page; the Keyboard list makes it longer still
Open Page

Panel D

A popover that drops from the strip, like Control Center. The six settings people flip most sit up top as tiles, and each section shows a one-line summary of its state. A section opens in place with a back button, and the window stays visible beside it.

Navigation
Arrow keys walk the list; → or Return opens; ← goes back
Strength
Fastest for quick changes; summaries show state without opening anything
Tradeoff
Narrow; Keyboard and Auto-cleanup widen the panel to fit
Open Panel

Things to try in every design

  • ⌘, opens and closes settings; Esc clears search, then closes
  • ⌘F searches; try “trash”, “dark”, “rename”, or “node”
  • Keyboard: click New Folder’s shortcut and press ⌘D to see a conflict
  • Turn on Vim mode, then try to record j
  • Change the theme, accent, or row height and watch the preview (or, in B, the whole window)
  • Change something, hover it, and click the restore arrow
  • Drag pinned folders to reorder them
  • Auto-cleanup: change the days and watch the forecast update

Demo states also load from the URL, for example a-sheet.html#section=keyboard&theme=dark, q=trash, vim, conflict=newFolder:cmd+d, or sidebar=right.
